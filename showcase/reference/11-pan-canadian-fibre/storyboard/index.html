What is real in these frames

  • Every data layer is the planner's own file from data/layers, drawn with the planner's layer names and feature counts.
  • The fifteen routes are the planner's precomputed solver results, with their real lengths.
  • The three Alberta-to-Pacific routes were solved by the running planner's own API for this film. The search animation replays the order in which the solver settled each node.
  • The Mackenzie Valley estimate is the planner's own answer for a line drawn along its "not routed" stretch.
  • The panel is a dark re-draw of the real side panel: same title, tabs, groups, labels, buttons and warnings.
  • The planet is NASA Blue Marble relief and Black Marble night lights, with 3D terrain from open elevation tiles. The planner itself uses a street map; the imagery is the one substitution, made for the cinema look, and it is credited on screen.

What I need from you

  1. The closing line. The Agency rules reserve conclusions for the owner. Frame 15 carries a working draft built from your brief.
  2. The Alberta beat in frame 4. "Alberta has the energy and no coast" is my framing of your brief. The data shows the power lines, pipelines and plants; it does not measure AI demand.
  3. The planning example. I chose three routes from Alberta to the Pacific. Say so if another corridor tells the story better.
  4. The tool's caveats. The panel keeps the real warnings: interim sample network, costs hidden, estimate indicative. I recommend keeping them on screen and out of the narration, except "indicative".
  5. Classification and captions. I assumed Unclassified and burned-in captions.
