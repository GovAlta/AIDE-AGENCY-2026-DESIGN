# Writing in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English: the rules this skill applies

ASD-STE100 is the aerospace and defence controlled language for technical documents: one word for one meaning, short
sentences, active voice. The full specification and its dictionary are published by ASD (asd-ste100.org). This page is a
working summary for our reports, and `scripts/ste_check.py` checks the measurable parts of it. The checker approximates the
standard: it does not hold the licensed dictionary, so a human reads the final text too.

## Words

1. Use **one word for one meaning**, and the same word every time: do not switch between "brief", "spec" and "package
   description" for one thing.
2. Prefer the **approved simple word**: use, not utilize; about, not approximately; more, not additional; make sure, not
   ensure; start, not commence; help, not facilitate; show, not demonstrate; get, not obtain; tell, not inform.
3. Use a word only with its **approved meaning and part of speech**: "test" is a noun and a verb; do not use a verb as a
   noun when the noun exists.
4. **Technical names and technical verbs** are permitted: program names (LTAU8225), table names, "migrate", "deploy",
   "merge", "compile", when the reader's field uses them.
5. Do not use **phrasal verbs** ("set up", "carry out"): use one verb (prepare, do).
6. Write **articles** ("the", "a") and **"that"** where they make the sentence clear.

## Sentences

7. A **procedural sentence** (an instruction) has **20 words or fewer**. A **descriptive sentence** has **25 words or fewer**.
8. Write **one instruction per sentence**, in the **imperative**: "Rotate the credentials."
9. Use the **active voice**: "The program posts the deposit", not "The deposit is posted by the program". Passive voice is
   permitted only when the actor is not known or does not matter, and only in descriptive text.
10. Use the **simple tenses** (present, past, future). Do not use "-ing" forms as verbs or to start a sentence.
11. Write the **condition first**: "If the deposit is not pending, refuse the close."
12. Do not use **semicolons** to join sentences; write two sentences.

## Paragraphs and structure

13. A paragraph has **one topic** and **no more than 6 sentences**.
14. Put the **important information first**: the result, then the detail.
15. Use **vertical lists** for steps and for items in parallel.
16. Write **warnings and cautions** before the step they apply to, with the reason.

## In our reports

- Part 1 of a report ("Context and intent") is written for non-technical readers in plain language. It may break rule 7
  where a longer sentence reads better, and the checker skips it (`ste_skip_parts: [1]`).
- Every other part follows the rules above.
- Numbers keep their units; codes and names stay exactly as in the source.
